# Protos Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Protos welcomes people who want to use, study, question, improve, or build with
the language.

Participation is welcome regardless of experience level, background, identity,
employer, whether a contribution is open-source or commercial, or whether the
work was written manually, AI-assisted, or fully AI-generated.

The project expects the same thing from everyone: constructive participation
that makes it easier to understand the problem, evaluate evidence, and improve
the project.

## Expected behavior

Please:

- be respectful and specific when disagreeing;
- critique ideas, designs, code, and evidence rather than people;
- give newcomers room to ask basic questions;
- assume good faith while still checking technical claims;
- make uncertainty explicit instead of presenting guesses as facts;
- keep discussions relevant enough that other participants can follow them; and
- help turn disagreements into testable questions, design choices, or concrete
  next steps.

Strong technical disagreement is welcome. Personal attacks are not.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following is not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or sustained personal hostility;
- discriminatory or demeaning comments;
- sexualized attention or content directed at another participant;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- deliberately disrupting discussions, issues, reviews, or project
  infrastructure;
- misrepresenting another contributor's work or review; or
- attacking or dismissing a contribution solely because AI was or was not used
  to produce it.

Concerns about generated code should be expressed as ordinary engineering
concerns: correctness, provenance, licensing, security, maintainability,
specification compliance, review quality, or missing evidence.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies to repository issues, pull requests, Discussions,
and other spaces presented as official Protos community spaces.

## Moderation

Maintainers may edit or remove content, close or lock conversations, or restrict
participation when necessary to keep project spaces constructive and safe.

When possible, moderation should explain the relevant behavior rather than turn
technical or interpersonal disagreement into a judgment about a person's
motives.

For an ordinary moderation concern, use GitHub's reporting tools or raise the
concern with the maintainer in the relevant project space. If a report contains
private or sensitive information, do not publish those details in a public
issue or Discussion; request a private reporting channel instead.
